# EdgeLog Marketing & Positioning

This document is the working source of truth for EdgeLog's external positioning, product story, and marketing language. Product behavior remains governed by the product specification and scoped GitHub issues.

## Brand

**EdgeLog**

Primary brand phrase:

> **Find Your Edge.**

## Core positioning

> **Built by traders, for traders. A serious trading journal designed to help you find your edge, understand your triggers, and build barriers against tilt.**

EdgeLog is not positioned as another passive broker-import dashboard. The act of journaling is part of the discipline.

> **You traded it. You journal it. You own it.**

EdgeLog should make that work more valuable, structured, and insightful — not eliminate it.

## Three product pillars

### 1. FIND YOUR EDGE

Define the setups you actually trade. Journal them deliberately. Review the outcomes. Use your own data to determine what works for **you**.

EdgeLog does not claim there is one universal A+ setup. The trader defines the edge; EdgeLog helps test it against the trader's own history.

**Define setup → journal trades → measure outcomes → discover edge.**

### 2. KNOW YOUR TRIGGERS

Trading performance is not only setup quality. EdgeLog should help traders identify the situations, thoughts, emotions, and urges that precede poor decisions.

The CBT-informed Tilt Journal supports structured reflection around:

**Trigger → Automatic Thought → Emotion → Urge → Behavior → Consequence → Challenge/Reframe → Recovery Action → Later Reflection**

The goal is not diagnosis or therapy. The goal is to help a trader recognize their own recurring decision-making patterns.

### 3. BUILD YOUR BARRIERS

Insight should lead to action.

As EdgeLog learns from the trader's deliberately journaled history, it should help the trader turn recurring patterns into **precommitted personal barriers** against known failure modes.

Examples of the concept:

- After two stop-outs, step away before another trade.
- When revenge-trade urge reaches a personally defined threshold, trigger a cooldown routine.
- After a missed entry, reinforce the trader's no-chasing rule.
- When a recurring trigger appears, surface the trader's own predetermined response.

These barriers should ultimately be based on the trader's own rules and observed patterns. EdgeLog should support discipline, not pretend to replace the trader's judgment.

## Product loops

### Day-to-day workflow

**Plan → Trade → Journal → Reflect → Improve**

### Longer-term improvement loop

**Define → Journal → Discover → Protect → Improve**

The first describes how a trader uses EdgeLog around trading sessions. The second describes how EdgeLog helps turn accumulated journal data into a stronger process over time.

## Manual-first differentiation

Manual journaling is intentional product value.

EdgeLog should reduce **clerical friction** without removing **reflective friction**.

- Broker/execution data may eventually prefill objective facts.
- Screenshot/AI tools may suggest candidate information.
- The trader confirms setup, context, execution quality, psychology, mistakes, and reflection.
- EdgeLog should not silently turn imported executions into completed journal entries.
- Analytics are downstream of deliberate journaling.

The underlying belief is simple: serious improvement requires a trader to confront and review their own decisions.

## Tone

EdgeLog marketing should feel:

- serious
- disciplined
- trader-native
- direct
- credible
- process-oriented
- premium without being flashy

Avoid:

- get-rich-quick language
- promises of profitability
- "AI finds winning trades" claims
- generic hustle/motivation copy
- casino/gambling imagery
- Wall Street clichés
- bulls/bears/money graphics
- presenting CBT-informed reflection as psychotherapy or mental-health treatment

## Messaging hierarchy

When explaining EdgeLog to a new trader:

1. **Find Your Edge** — discover what actually works from your own deliberately journaled trades.
2. **Know Your Triggers** — understand the patterns that damage execution and discipline.
3. **Build Your Barriers** — turn those patterns into personal rules and interventions before tilt takes over.
4. Explain the manual-first philosophy: the journal is part of the work, not administrative overhead to automate away.
5. Only then introduce specific features such as Trade Ranker, Tilt Journal, analytics, screenshots/AI assistance, or future execution integration.

## Current feature-to-message mapping

- **Daily Journal:** deliberate preparation, trade capture, and review.
- **Trade Ranker / Define Your Edge:** trader-defined setups and evidence about what actually works.
- **Tilt Journal:** CBT-informed structured reflection for identifying triggers, urges, behaviors, and recovery patterns.
- **Future Tilt Insights:** identify recurring personal patterns and measure successful interruption/catch rate.
- **Future Barriers:** convert known triggers/patterns into trader-defined precommitments and interventions.
- **Strategy Trader:** future execution assistance remains subordinate to the journal/process philosophy; knowing an execution occurred must not silently complete the trader's reflection.

## Naming note

Keep **EdgeLog** as the product name unless a later branding decision explicitly changes it. "Hedge Log" can imply a journal specifically for hedging activity and does not communicate the broader product as clearly.

## Positioning boundary

EdgeLog helps traders improve their **process**. Marketing should not promise that using EdgeLog will make someone profitable or guarantee better trading outcomes.

CBT concepts are described as **CBT-informed structured reflection**. EdgeLog is not psychotherapy, does not diagnose mental-health conditions, and should not market itself as treatment.
